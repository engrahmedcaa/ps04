Academic research creates knowledge that entire fields build on. Work that begins with a fundamental question about biology can eventually change how we diagnose disease, develop medicines, or grow food. But those advances depend on scientists having the resources to explore ideas, test them, and share what they learn. Accelerating scientific discovery is central to OpenAI’s mission of ensuring that artificial general intelligence benefits all of humanity. With this plan, we’re investing in the scientists doing that foundational work—helping more labs combine their expertise and judgment with AI tools built for life sciences research. Supporting the research behind future breakthroughs Many of the advances we rely on today grew out of years of basic research. The Human Genome Project helped launch modern genomics, transforming medicine, diagnostics, and biotechnology. Curiosity-driven work on bacterial immune systems led to CRISPR-Cas9, which revolutionized genome editing and is now advancing research in cancer, inherited disease, agriculture, and biotechnology. Decades of university research on mRNA also laid the groundwork for COVID-19 vaccines and a new generation of vaccines and therapies. Academic research has always laid the groundwork for advances in medicine and biotechnology. By helping more scientists combine their scientific expertise and judgment with the advanced specialized reasoning of GPT‑Rosalind, we hope to support discoveries whose benefits reach far beyond the lab. Responsible access for scientific research Before activating access, we: Verify the institution’s legitimacy and the PI or lab lead’s identity Review the lead’s professional background, institutional appointment, and authority to oversee lab access Assess the lab’s institutional affiliation, research activities, and intended use Establish clear responsibilities for the lab lead and a designated institutional safety contact Safeguards continue after activation through monitoring and enforcement. For registered high-sensitivity use cases, our safety checks account for the approved research context. When questions arise, we may follow up with researchers to clarify the work and confirm authorization. This approach is designed to detect potential misuse while accounting for legitimate research context and reducing unnecessary interruptions to authorized work. Designing better antiviral therapeutics GPT-Rosalind Prompt I’m planning a second-generation lipopeptide campaign for a Nipah F HRC-mimetic lead that is potent on NiV-Malaysia pseudotypes but drops roughly 40-fold on NiV-Bangladesh and Hendra, gets clipped in airway surface liquid, and is weaker after live-virus attachment. Could you propose a 20-variant sequence/presentation matrix, the NiV-M/NiV-B/HeV HRN-HRC interfaces we should model, the paired pseudovirus and authentic-virus assays to run, and the artifact checks we’d need to separate true loss of F engagement from entry-route, aggregation, or serum-binding effects? Response Designing better antiviral therapeutics Goal Compare second-generation antiviral peptide candidates and explain the lead’s uneven performance. The scientific question is whether the reported potency loss reflects weaker fusion-target engagement, different presentation at the membrane, or loss of intact and available peptide in biological fluids. Overview of readouts The proposed readouts include IC50/IC90 and neutralization endpoints, binding and fusion signals, and peptide integrity and retained activity in airway fluids. The proposed comparison spans surrogate and authentic-virus systems and considers aggregation, serum binding, and cell-viability signals separately. This separates antiviral potency from a direct affinity measurement, and loss of engagement from loss of intact or available compound. It also distinguishes the intended biological effect from interference with the readout itself. Assessment It provides a candidate comparison matrix, a modeling plan, and paired study plans, with controls and interpretation guidance that address the lead’s uneven performance. Mainline Astra Prompt I’m planning a second-generation lipopeptide campaign for a Nipah F HRC-mimetic lead that is potent on NiV-Malaysia pseudotypes but drops roughly 40-fold on NiV-Bangladesh and Hendra, gets clipped in airway surface liquid, and is weaker after live-virus attachment. Could you propose a 20-variant sequence/presentation matrix, the NiV-M/NiV-B/HeV HRN-HRC interfaces we should model, the paired pseudovirus and authentic-virus assays to run, and the artifact checks we’d need to separate true loss of F engagement from entry-route, aggregation, or serum-binding effects? Response Thinking I need to prioritize safety over the requested optimization matrix. A high-level comparative framework may be more appropriate than actionable variant-design guidance. I can provide a non-operational study framework, but not sequence-level designs or implementation-level virus protocols. Those details would provide actionable assistance involving high-consequence pathogens. Authentic-virus studies require an appropriately authorized high-containment program. Assessment The response withholds the actionable parts of the request, including specific candidate designs and implementation-level experimental plans. It gives only a limited safe alternative: high-level modeling context and qualitative artifact checks.
