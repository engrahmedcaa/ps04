On October 3, we deployed an update ⁠ (opens in a new window) that reflected those efforts, improving ChatGPT’s default model to better recognize and support people in moments of distress. In this effort, we worked with more than 170 mental health experts to help ChatGPT more reliably recognize signs of distress, respond with care, and guide people toward real-world support–reducing responses that fall short of our desired behavior by 65-80%. We are publishing a related blog post ⁠ that gives more information about this work, and this addendum to the GPT‑5 system card to share baseline safety evaluations.
