# Editorial policy: Small-Team Agent Field Notes

- **Audience and relevance:** prioritize evidence that helps a small AI application team decide whether, how, or when to adopt an agent framework or tool. Consider integration effort, maintenance burden, observability, evaluation, reliability, security, latency, and total cost. Reject generic model announcements without a concrete agent-development implication.
- **Freshness:** require a verifiable original publication date within the run's inclusive 30-day UTC window. Unknown or future dates are ineligible.
- **Credibility:** assess each claim using inspectable evidence, methodology, task selection, sample size, baselines, reproducibility, version information, and independent corroboration. Separate vendor documentation and benchmark claims from independently measured results and developer experience. Reputation and model confidence alone are insufficient.
- **Novelty:** distinguish a genuinely new finding from a repost, unchanged page with a refreshed date, or old event repackaged as news.
- **Perspective:** seek technical documentation/research, commercial announcements, and practitioner reports. Prefer evidence spanning more than one provider ecosystem. Count publishers and underlying claims; mirrors, reposts, and syndicated copies are not independent.
- **Uncertainty and conflict:** distinguish prototype, benchmark, and production evidence. Preserve disagreements, name missing information, describe what could resolve it, and defer claims that cannot be responsibly represented.
- **Commercial incentives:** disclose vendor interests and treat announcements as claims until independently supported.
- **Adoption caution:** do not recommend a framework solely because it is popular or has a polished demo. Ask whether the evidence addresses failure recovery, tool permissions, prompt injection, human handoff, tracing, reproducible evaluation, version compatibility, and operating cost where relevant.
- **Exclusions:** promotional material with no inspectable evidence, duplicate episodes, undated content, unsupported causal claims, old events relabeled as new, and material outside the stated developer audience and scope.

AI output is triage. A human reviewer must verify source passages, dates, independence, interpretations, and corrections before approval.
